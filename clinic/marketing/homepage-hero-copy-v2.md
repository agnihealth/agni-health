## Problem with Current Hero

The current homepage hero (as analyzed in Phase 2 brief) likely leads with brand identity (Agni Health, metabolic health) rather than speaking directly to the visitor's felt experience. The winning ad drove them to the page by activating a specific pain — the homepage needs to *continue* that conversation.

---

### Option B — "Your family history" frame

**H1**:
> Your father had diabetes.  
> Your A1c is 5.6%.  
> This is not a coincidence — and it's not your destiny.

**Subheadline**:
> Agni Health is physician-led telehealth for South Asian men. We use the labs and thresholds that are actually calibrated for our biology — not borrowed from Western population studies.

**CTA Button**:

> *$350 | CA, MA, LA | HSA/FSA accepted*

---

## Recommendation

**Lead with Option A.** It directly mirrors Ad 4's winning copy ("Take control of your blood sugar") and speaks to the most common scenario: a patient who got a "borderline" result and was dismissed. Option B is stronger for someone who hasn't seen their labs yet — potentially a retargeting or blog-driven audience.

**A/B test**: If Vercel supports edge-based A/B testing (it does via Vercel Edge Config or middleware), run A vs. B for 2 weeks once traffic reaches ~200 sessions/week.

### FAQ Section (Address Conversion Objections)

**Q: Does insurance cover this?**  
A: Agni Health is direct-pay. We don't bill insurance. HSA and FSA cards are accepted. Many patients find the out-of-pocket cost comparable to multiple co-pays for incomplete workups.

**Q: What states are you seeing patients in?**  
A: Currently California, Louisiana, and Massachusetts. If you're elsewhere, join the waitlist — we're expanding.

**Q: What happens during the first visit?**  
A: 30-minute video consultation. We review your history, family risk, existing labs, and order a comprehensive SA-specific metabolic panel. You'll receive a written summary and your personalized protocol within 48 hours.

**Q: How is this different from my regular doctor?**  
A: Most PCPs use population-level reference ranges built on white, Western cohorts. We use South Asian-specific thresholds for BMI, waist circumference, A1c, and ApoB. For South Asian men, these differences are clinically meaningful.

**Q: Is this just about losing weight?**  
A: No. Metabolic health is about insulin sensitivity, lipid particle quality, inflammation, and cardiovascular risk — most of which don't correlate cleanly with the number on your scale. Some of our highest-risk patients have a "normal" BMI.
