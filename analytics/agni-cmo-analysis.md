# Agni Health — CMO Strategic Assessment
**Campaign:** $500 Pilot May 2026 (Actual Spend: $745.46)  
**Period:** May 6–16, 2026  
**Analyst:** Clinic CMO Subagent  
**Date:** May 16, 2026

---

## Executive Summary

This pilot campaign delivered **exceptional top-of-funnel efficiency** ($0.23 CPC, 5.79% CTR) but suffered **catastrophic mid-funnel conversion failure**. The 96.9% homepage bounce rate signals a fundamental disconnect between ad promise and landing page delivery. We generated 56K impressions and 1,269 landing page views but converted only 5 leads—a $149 cost per lead that is unsustainable for a health coaching business.

**The verdict:** Our ads are working. Our website is not.

---

## 1. Creative Performance Assessment

### Winners

**Ad 6 (Three Stats) — Clear Winner on Efficiency**
- CTR: **10.48–10.50%** (nearly 2x campaign average)
- CPC: **$0.11–$0.16** (50% below average)
- Combined spend: $134.36 | LPVs: 182
- **Why it works:** Specific, quantified health claims create curiosity and credibility. Numbers ("3 stats") promise concrete value without requiring emotional buy-in.

**Ad 7 (Belly Fat Video) — Volume Leader**
- Highest spend allocation: $172.61
- CTR: 5.45% (solid)
- LPVs: 369 (highest absolute volume)
- **Why it works:** Video format + visceral pain point (belly fat) captures attention in scroll. Strong hook potential.

### Underperformers

**Ad 4 (Blood Sugar Copy)**
- CTR: 3.26% (37% below average)
- CPC: $0.41 (78% above average)
- **Why it fails:** "Blood sugar" may feel clinical/scary, attracting only diagnosed diabetics rather than the broader metabolic health audience.

**Ad 5 Before/After (A test copy)**
- Lower CTR (4.63%) and higher CPC ($0.29) than B test
- Only 64 LPVs despite being a proven format
- **Why it underperformed:** Copy variation matters. The B test copy outperformed, suggesting the framing/hook was the differentiator.

### Key Creative Insight
**Specificity wins.** The "Three Stats" creative with concrete numbers dramatically outperformed vague health messaging. The audience responds to quantified, curiosity-driven hooks over emotional or fear-based appeals.

## 2. Audience Signal Read

### What the Data Tells Us

**High CTR (5.79%) + Low CPC ($0.23) = Strong Audience-Message Fit**
- This CTR is 2–3x typical health/wellness benchmarks
- The audience Meta is finding is genuinely interested in metabolic health content
- Frequency of 1.21x means we're not burning out the audience yet

**The Checkout Signal is Concerning**
- Only 2 checkout initiations from 1,269 landing page views
- 0.16% LPV-to-checkout rate is critically low
- This isn't an audience problem—it's a conversion problem

**Audience Resonance Pattern**
| Signal | Reading |
|--------|---------|
| 10%+ CTR on stats-based ads | Audience wants education, not fear |
| Video performing well | Audience engages with visual content |
| Belly fat > Blood sugar | Cosmetic concerns may outweigh clinical concerns |
| Before/After works | Social proof and transformation stories resonate |

### Audience Hypothesis
Your best audience is **health-curious, pre-clinical**—people who want to optimize, not people managing diagnosed conditions. They respond to specific, measurable outcomes rather than disease-focused messaging.

## 3. Homepage Conversion Problem — The Critical Diagnosis

### The Numbers Tell the Story

```
1,511 sessions → 55 reach /start (3.6%) → 20 reach /book (1.3%) → 5 leads (0.33%)
```

**96.9% of visitors never leave the homepage.** This is the campaign's fatal flaw.

### Root Cause Analysis

**Primary Diagnosis: Message-to-Page Mismatch**

The ads promise specific, quantified health insights ("Three stats," transformation stories). The homepage likely delivers:
- Generic health coaching positioning
- No immediate continuation of the ad's promise
- Unclear next step or value proposition

**Secondary Factors:**

1. **No conversion-optimized landing page exists.** We're sending ad traffic to a general homepage, not a campaign-specific LP that continues the ad narrative.

2. **Friction at /start.** Only 3.6% even attempt the first conversion action. The CTA is either:
   - Not visible above the fold
   - Not compelling enough
   - Asking for too much too soon

3. **Mobile experience likely broken.** With ad traffic being predominantly mobile, any mobile UX issues compound rapidly.

4. **Trust gap.** New brand, no recognizable credibility markers, health is high-stakes—visitors bounce before investing attention.

### The Math Problem

At $149/lead and likely needing 20+ leads to close one customer, we're looking at **$3,000+ customer acquisition cost**. Unless LTV exceeds $3K, this funnel is structurally unprofitable.

## 4. Recommended Next Steps — Top 3 Actions

### Action 1: Build Dedicated Landing Pages (CRITICAL)

**Do this before spending another dollar on ads.**

Create 2-3 landing pages that:
- Continue the specific ad narrative (e.g., "Here are the 3 stats that predict metabolic dysfunction")
- Deliver immediate value before asking for anything
- Have ONE clear CTA above the fold
- Include trust signals: credentials, testimonials, media mentions

**Test structure:** Ad → LP with lead magnet → Email nurture → Book call

### Action 2: Implement a Lead Magnet Funnel

Current ask: Book a call (high commitment)  
Better ask: Get [specific free resource] (low commitment)

Options:
- "Get Your 3-Number Metabolic Risk Score" (interactive quiz)
- "The 5-Minute Belly Fat Assessment" (PDF + email capture)
- "Free Metabolic Health Masterclass" (video series)

**Goal:** Convert the 96.9% bouncing visitors into an email list you can nurture at zero marginal cost.

### Action 3: Install Conversion Tracking + Heatmaps

Before next campaign:
- Verify Meta pixel fires correctly at each funnel stage
- Install Hotjar/FullStory on homepage to see where users drop
- Set up proper UTM tracking to attribute leads to specific ads
- Create PostHog funnels for each ad → LP → conversion path

## 5. Budget Allocation Recommendation

### If Running Another $500–$750 Campaign

**Do NOT run more ads until landing pages are fixed.** Another $750 into this funnel will yield ~5 more leads at $149 each. That's burning money.

### When Ready to Resume (Post-LP Fix)

| Allocation | Amount | Rationale |
|------------|--------|-----------|
| **Stats-based creative variations** | 40% (~$300) | Scale what works. Test "5 stats," "7 signs," etc. |
| **Video creative (belly fat angle)** | 30% (~$225) | High volume potential, test different hooks |
| **Before/After with B-test copy style** | 20% (~$150) | Proven format, optimize copy |
| **New test creative** | 10% (~$75) | Test one new angle (e.g., energy/fatigue) |

### Audience Allocation
- Keep current targeting but exclude 1x visitors who didn't convert (they've seen and rejected)
- Build lookalike from the 5 waitlist signups once you have 25+
- Test interest stacking: metabolic health + specific wellness interests

### Structural Recommendation

**Shift from "campaign" thinking to "funnel" thinking.**

Budget split should be:
- 30% on acquisition (Meta ads)
- 40% on conversion infrastructure (LP, lead magnets, email sequences)
- 30% on measurement/optimization (tracking, testing tools)

Right now we're 100% acquisition, 0% conversion infrastructure. That ratio is inverted from where it should be.
