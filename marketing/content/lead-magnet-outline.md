## Page 1 — The Problem (Hook)
**Headline:** "Your tests came back normal. But something still feels off."

South Asian men are developing prediabetes and metabolic disease at twice the rate of their White peers — often *despite* healthier diets and more exercise. By age 45, nearly 1 in 3 South Asian men has prediabetes.

The problem: standard guidelines were built on Western European populations. The thresholds are wrong. The tests are incomplete.

This guide gives you what most doctors don't know to look for.

## Page 2 — The South Asian Metabolic Paradox
**Headline:** "Healthier habits. Worse outcomes. Why?"

Key points:
- South Asians carry more visceral fat at lower BMI
- Insulin resistance develops earlier and at lower weight
- Standard BMI cutoff of 25 misses many at-risk South Asian men (ethnic threshold: 23)
- The MASALA Study (largest US research on South Asian cardiovascular risk) documented this in detail

> *"A South Asian man at BMI 24 may have the cardiovascular risk of a White man at BMI 28."*

## Page 3 — The 3 Tests Your Doctor Probably Didn't Order
**Headline:** "A1c is a lagging indicator. Here's what to ask for instead."

### 1. Fasting Insulin
- Normal range: 2–6 µIU/mL (most labs say "normal" up to 25)
- What it shows: how hard your pancreas is working before blood sugar rises
- The canary in the coal mine — shows insulin resistance years before A1c moves

### 2. HOMA-IR
- Calculated from fasting glucose + fasting insulin
- Threshold for concern: > 2.0 (standard labs don't even report this)
- South Asian men often show HOMA-IR > 3.5 with "normal" A1c

### 3. ApoB
- Better predictor of cardiovascular risk than LDL
- South Asians often have high ApoB with "normal" LDL (small dense LDL particles)
- Optimal: < 80 mg/dL

## Page 5 — The Self-Assessment
**Headline:** "Where do you stand?"

**Circle the items that apply:**
- [ ] A1c between 5.5–5.9 ("normal" or "borderline")
- [ ] Fasting glucose 95–115 mg/dL
- [ ] BMI 22–26 with belly fat you can't lose
- [ ] Parent or sibling with type 2 diabetes
- [ ] Triglycerides > 150
- [ ] Fatigue after meals
- [ ] Difficulty losing weight despite exercise
- [ ] High-stress, sedentary job (even if you work out)

**Scoring:**
- 0–1: Lower risk — still worth monitoring with the right tests
- 2–3: Moderate risk — request the 8 markers at your next physical
- 4+: High risk — South Asian-specific metabolic evaluation strongly recommended
