- South Asian men: **30.7%**

**Title:** "South Asian men: your A1c might be 'normal' and you might still have significant metabolic risk — here's what to actually ask your doctor"

Hi — I'm an internal medicine physician who specializes in South Asian metabolic health. I wanted to share some information that I think is underrepresented in how we talk about our community's health.

**The stats that should be talked about more:**

~1 in 3 South Asian men has prediabetes by age 45. That's 8x the rate of White peers — and this is *despite* South Asians generally reporting healthier diets and exercise habits. This is from the MASALA Study, the largest US research on South Asian cardiovascular risk.

**Why standard guidelines miss this:**

The A1c thresholds, BMI cutoffs, and metabolic normal ranges we use in standard medicine were largely developed on Western European populations. South Asians:
- Carry more visceral fat at lower BMI (risk threshold = 23, not 25)
- Develop insulin resistance earlier and at lower body weight
- Often have concerning HOMA-IR and fasting insulin when A1c is "normal"

**What to ask your doctor:**

If you're South Asian, male, and 35+, consider requesting:
- **Fasting insulin** (not just fasting glucose or A1c)
- **HOMA-IR** (calculated from fasting glucose + insulin)
- **ApoB** (better than LDL for predicting CV risk in South Asians)
- Waist circumference measured against ethnic-specific thresholds

Many PCPs aren't familiar with the South Asian-specific literature — it's not a criticism, it's just not covered well in standard training. You can be your own advocate.
